Private anxiety and mindfulness apps: which keep your data on your device (2026)

Every App Store page carries a privacy label the developer has to declare. We read fourteen of them, panic, grounding, breathing, and meditation apps, so you don't have to. The short version: Calm, Balance, Breathwrk, Rootd, Dare, and Mellow declare "Data Used to Track You"; Headspace, Finch, Insight Timer, and MindShift collect data linked to you; and exactly four apps, Reground, PanicShield, Anchor, and NerveCalm, say "Data Not Collected". Labels checked on the US App Store, August 2026.

How we read the labels, and how we picked the apps

Apple requires every app to declare what it collects, in four categories, from worst to best: Data Used to Track You (shared with data brokers or used for cross-app advertising), Data Linked to You (collected and tied to your identity), Data Not Linked to You (collected, kept anonymous), and Data Not Collected. An app can carry several at once; we list each app under the strongest category it declares. We picked apps people actually get recommended for panic and anxiety, plus the big meditation subscriptions for contrast. A disclosure: we make Reground, one of the apps below. The labels are Apple's, not ours, open any listing and check.

Why the label matters more here than in most categories

The moment you open a panic app is, almost by definition, a moment you'd rather keep to yourself. What you write in a worry journal, when your panic spikes, how often, that's mental-health data, some of the most sensitive data a phone can hold. "Data Used to Track You" on a panic app means some of your data feeds advertising systems owned by other companies. That's not a scandal, it's a business model, but it's worth knowing before you type your 3 a.m. thoughts into one. Are App Store privacy labels trustworthy?

They're self-declared, so treat them as a floor, not a guarantee. A developer who lies on the label violates App Store rules and risks removal, which is real pressure, but nobody audits every app. The strongest signal is architecture: an app with no account, no server, and offline operation has nothing to collect with. That's the standard we hold grounding apps to as well, and the reason Reground's label isn't a promise, it's a description.

Which anxiety apps don't collect any data?

Four apps in this comparison carry the App Store "Data Not Collected" label: Reground ($5.99 once), PanicShield (free), Anchor: Anxiety Release (free), and NerveCalm ($29.99/yr). Every other app we checked, including Calm, Headspace, Rootd, Dare, Breathwrk, Balance, Finch, and Insight Timer, declares that it collects data in some form. US App Store labels, August 2026.

What does "Data Used to Track You" mean on the App Store?

It means the developer declares that some of your data, often identifiers or usage data, is shared with data brokers or used to follow you across other companies' apps and websites, typically for advertising. It's the strongest of Apple's privacy label categories, and in August 2026 it appears on Calm, Balance, Breathwrk, Rootd, Dare, and Mellow.

Do Calm and Headspace track you?

By their own App Store labels, August 2026: Calm declares "Data Used to Track You" and "Data Linked to You". Headspace declares "Data Linked to You" but not the tracking category. Neither is anonymous; the difference is whether data leaves for other companies' advertising.

Can a free anxiety app be private?

Yes, but don't assume it. PanicShield and Anchor are free and labeled "Data Not Collected". MindShift CBT is free but declares "Data Linked to You". Free often means the data is the price, so check the App Privacy section of the listing rather than the price tag.
